The mark & the mascot

Icon: a trawl net, mouth open, hauling upward. Geometric and strokes-only, so it still reads at 18 px and 11 px (shown right). Ships square, round and monochrome.

Mascot: a fish — the catch, not the catcher. D-04 deliberately kept every trace of "Seal" out of this project, so a seal, otter or walrus mascot would walk that straight back to the one thing upstream asked forks not to do. A fish is cute, obviously on-theme, and shares nothing with the name Trawl replaced. It lives in empty states and the tail of the intro — never in the launcher icon, where a face turns to mush at 16 px.

What's wrong with the current design

  1. 1
    ≈120 px of the first screen is a logo. Trawl's header does the same job in ~52 px, and switches off.
  2. 2
    The placeholder animates forever. URLInputField runs an infinite gradient shift on the hint text.
  3. 3
    The "glassmorphism" is not glass. GlassCard takes a blurRadius that appears nowhere in its body.
  4. 4
    A pulsing gradient "+". AnimatedGlowingPlus, breathing on a 1.6 s loop.
  5. 5
    Bouncy spring on every tap. 0.88 scale on DampingRatioMediumBouncy, on every control.
  6. 6
    ~2,400 lines of donation surface, including a dialog 600 ms after the home screen loads.

Round log

Round 6 — manual switching, context-aware bar

  • "Keep switcher open" (Settings). With it on, picking a window only makes it active: the menu stays up and the shrunken card previews that screen, ringed in the accent colour with a tap the window to open it hint. Tapping the card enters. Select, then enter — the way a real task switcher behaves.
  • This needed a state fix, not a flag. "Which screen is showing" and "is the switcher open" had been the same variable (screen === 'drawer'), which makes previewing impossible by construction — you cannot be on Settings and in the switcher at once. They are separate now, which also fixed a quieter bug: closing the switcher used to dump you on Home instead of leaving you where you were.
  • The history button behaves like the gear. Both replay the full switcher move — open, land, zoom in — rather than teleporting, and both have their own on/off switch.
  • Shortcuts hide on the screen they point at. No gear while you are in Settings, no clock while you are in Links history. A shortcut to where you already are is clutter.
  • The menu highlights the active window, so in pinned mode you can see which one the card is previewing without reading it.
  • A refresh never restores a half-open switcher — drawerOpen is forced false on load, so a saved mid-transition state cannot strand the page.

Round 5 — window switcher, About page, mascot

  • The drawer is a window switcher now, and moves like one. Picking a destination swaps the card's contents while it is still pushed back and small, then zooms it in. It reads as changing windows rather than as a menu closing and a page appearing.
  • The X is gone. Tapping the pushed-back card is the only way back, which is what the reference does and one less control on screen.
  • New app-bar button. The hamburger became a window-switcher glyph — two stacked cards — because it no longer opens a menu, it switches views. It nudges back and rotates slightly while the switcher is open.
  • The gear is gone from the top bar, with an opt-in to bring it back (Settings → Quick settings button). When it is on, tapping it plays the whole switcher move — opens, lands on Settings, zooms in — instead of teleporting there.
  • About page. A signature banner: the niccc2007 wordmark with a one-pass sheen, the mark as a watermark, an accent rule that draws itself in, and the mascot. Below it, the portfolio and GitHub links.
  • The upstream credit moved out of the menu and into About, where it finally has room to say what each project actually contributed. It moved — it did not go away. Crediting JunkFood02 and MaheshTechnicals is a stated requirement of this project, not just licence compliance, and "a fork, not an official build" stays with it.
  • Version line reads v0.1.0 · niccc2007, dropping the "fork of Seal Plus" tag from the menu header — that fact now lives on the About page where it belongs.
  • The mascot is visible and animated. In the intro it swims up out of the depths and flaps while it settles, offset from the mark instead of hiding behind the wordmark. It also caps the home list (that's the whole catch), sits on the About banner, and holds the empty states. One toggle in Settings turns all of it off; it also stops moving at Ambient motion: Off.

Round 4 — motion system, quick download, overlay permission

  • Two transition styles, switchable. Simple is the ordinary Material slide-over with a scrim. Fancy is the reference prototype's move: the whole page shrinks into a rounded card and is pushed off to the right, revealing a full-bleed menu behind it, items staggering in from the left. It is a flat scale + translate — no 3D rotation, because that is what the reference actually does; adding perspective looked showier and matched worse.
  • The style carries everywhere, not just the drawer: sheets rise with a slight scale, screen changes use a shared-axis slide, and the quick-download dialog pops with a spring. Simple reduces all of them to a short fade. One setting, one personality.
  • Tapping the pushed-back card closes the menu in Fancy mode — there is no scrim to tap, so the card itself is the way out, plus an X at the top of the menu column.
  • Quick download mirrors the app's real share-sheet entry. Upstream ships two share targets: MainActivity ("Download", the full app) and QuickDownloadActivity — a dialog-themed, singleInstance, excludeFromRecents activity labelled "Quick download" that floats the config sheet over whatever app you shared from. The mock's dialog is that one: source badge, the URL, resolved title, quality chips, and a More… escape to the full sheet.
  • You can start a download from the bubble now. Expand it and the first row is Paste a link, opening that same dialog — so the overlay is an entry point, not just a readout.
  • The overlay permission is a real gate. Turning the bubble on without it raises the "Display over other apps" request, which explains what it is for and states plainly that Trawl still works without it. Decline and the bubble stays off with progress in the notification; Settings shows a Permission needed badge until granted.
  • It is in the real manifest now, carrying the GPL-3 §5(a) "this file was modified" notice that D-02 requires — the first upstream file Trawl has changed, and the template for every one after. Verified: BUILD SUCCESSFUL, and the permission appears in the merged manifest.

Round 3 — identity, history, fast path, floating bubble

  • The app has a mark. A trawl net hauling upward, strokes-only, shown as square / round / monochrome launcher variants. It appears in the app bar, drawer, home header, intro and bubble — one shape doing all the identity work.
  • Mascot: a fish, not a mammal. Reasoning in the panel beside the phone. The catch, not the catcher.
  • Branded home header — mark + wordmark + fish, on by default, switchable off. ~52 px against Seal Plus's ~120 px, so you keep the branding without pushing the URL field under the fold.
  • "Show Seal Plus theme" toggle. Off removes it from the swatches and the top control row, and falls back to Ember if it was the active theme.
  • Links history — every link, with thumbnail, title, the URL itself and a status pill: Saved, Missing (file gone from disk) or Failed. The round button re-downloads at the remembered quality. Filters across the top; the mascot holds the empty state.
  • Fast download is genuinely faster. A tray drops out of the URL bar with three one-tap qualities and a More… escape to the full sheet. The FAST pill toggles it in place. Common case is now paste → one tap, no sheet round-trip.
  • The floating bubble. One conic-gradient ring per download, up to four concentric, then a count. Accent while running, green when all complete, red with a pulsing glow on error. Tap to expand into the task list with per-task progress, pause and retry. Drag it anywhere; an X rises from the bottom centre and swells as you approach — drop to dismiss. It also turns itself off from inside its own panel. On by default.
  • Queue handling: three run at once, the rest read Queued and promote automatically as slots free. The bubble's centre number counts everything unfinished, so it reads without expanding.
  • Why the bubble is a real Android feature, not chrome: it needs SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW ("Display over other apps"), a user-granted special permission. Trawl must degrade gracefully when it is refused — the notification already carries the same information, so the bubble is an enhancement, never the only way to see progress.
